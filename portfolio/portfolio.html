This is a portfolio page that explores different public policy data and visualises them using Vega-lite. The data sources are from government agencies, the World Bank and the IMF. The datasets, charts, and codes are available in my Github repository.

CC1: Charts about GDP

Country GDP vs Life Expectancy

Countries with higher GDP per capita tend to have higher life expectancy, and the biggest gains come at lower income levels: among wealthier countries, extra income makes smaller. European countries have life expectancy mostly above 75 years, while African countries are around 57 and 66 years. However, even countries with similar incomes can differ by 10 years or more in life expectancy. China and India, the two most populous countries, fall in the middle of the range rather than at either extreme.

GDP per capita, 1990-2022

Since 1990, incomes per person have roughly tripled in the US and UK and risen more than ten times in China. India has also grown quickly, while Nigeria and the DR. Congo have seen much slower progress.

GDP per Capita, 2022

In 2022, GDP per capita ranged from about $1,300 in the DR Congo to about $76,300 in the United States, a gap of roughly 57 times. China, at about $21,500, had the third highest GDP after the US and UK in 2022.

CC2: Charts about prices and public spending

US Weekly Diesel Prices, 1994-2026

US diesel prices stayed between about $1 and $1.50 per gallon through the 1990s, then rose through the 2000s to a peak near $4.80 in mid-2008, before falling to about $2 within a year. Prices stayed high, around $4, from 2011 to 2014, then dropped to about $2 again in early 2016. A second surge took prices to almost $5.80 in mid-2022, and they then eased to around $3.50 in 2025. The latest data show another sharp rise to about $6.50, the highest over the years.

UK Government Spending on Social Protection, 1997-2025

UK government spending on social protection services grew from about £38 billion in 1997 to about £68 billion in 2025. Spending rose quickly in the early 2000s, then stayed almost flat at around £50 billion from 2004 to 2016, before climbing steadily and then faster after 2021.

CC3: Policy debate

Should the UK prioritise job-search support over income support for under-25s on Universal Credit?

Universal Credit claimants by age

Under-25s are about 9% of claimants, so youth-specific support reaches a small share of the caseload.

Claimant conditionality regime by age

Over half of 16-19s are in the job-search regime, versus roughly one in six aged 35-44, so job-search support suits the youngest.
